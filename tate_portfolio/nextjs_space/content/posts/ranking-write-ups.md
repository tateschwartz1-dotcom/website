After a college Effective Altruism retreat, I found out that if I’m not on Substack, I’m only getting half the story.

To reintroduce the posts I’ve already published to my website, I’ll rank them:

# No. 5: [Why next year is a movie: Super El Niño vs. the World](/posts/super-el-nino)

In this one, I claim that the US Strategic Petroleum Reserve was on track to run out in October. The sky hasn’t fallen just yet.

The challenge with this post was expressing urgency while not sounding like a crazy doomsayer. People respond to specific concrete claims about the future, but getting one wrong discredits the already-precarious message.

I didn’t want to vaguely say “things will be very bad very soon” and get lost in the noise. I understand why climate scientists lose their minds.

# No. 4: [Why listen?](/posts/why-listen)

My goal with these posts is to not make them a diary for myself. If I’m going to publish something online, I want it to be useful to a stranger, not just a medium to process my thoughts.

This post was the one exception I let myself have. I felt the need to justify why I should be the one to write anything.

The kitchen visualization and list of ideas do sample my personality well. It’s a cute little two-minute read.

# No. 3: [My favorite beliefs](/posts/my-favorite-beliefs)

This is the variety show. Four acts of cool concepts are on display.

Although I love the topics, it’s missing that element to elevate it to the next level. For me, when writing, I don’t want to aim to be “good.” I aim to be different, out there, cutting through the noise and hooking the attention of the like-minded. A generic title like this will not be doing any of these activities.

Maybe this is the lovable filler episode.

Maybe saying that undersells it.

# No. 2: [Game design principles to use in real life](/posts/game-design-principles)

I hate prioritization. If I didn’t have ambition or reasoning, I would be failing all of my classes and spending my time publishing card games.

I love prioritization. Because I care so much about these posts being useful to all, I was able to take some of the abstract rules I followed and turn them into sound advice for myself.

This post was also practice for my love of concise storytelling. Out of everything I’ve written so far, this one is the most “me.”

# No. 1: [How to stare at something for 10 minutes](/posts/how-to-stare)

Now THIS has the je ne sais quoi that the first three posts on this list don’t have. A title that makes the eyes squint.

Lately, my staring sessions have been a lot more enjoyable because I knew I was doing research for this write-up. It has inspired me to start a document with a thought from every session moving forward. Maybe turning the staring into progress toward something enhances the experience. I’ll add this tip to the post if it works.

The strategies also apply to writing. A challenge: read one of my posts and sit with it during a five-minute timer. And donate me $20.
